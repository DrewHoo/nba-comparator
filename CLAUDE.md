# Project notes for the agent

- AGENTS.md lists the idiosyncrasies of the data and the logic the first build turned up (the two sources and what each lacks, the season naming, the All-Star/Cup/play-in rulings, team codes and the Charlotte trap, ids and names, positions, birth dates, who began before 1993-94, the columnar format). Read it before changing the ledger builder or the engine, and add to it when a gate or a reader teaches us a new one.

- What it is: a query engine for NBA career-point comparisons ("Zion Williamson has 2,524 points through his first 100 games"). A reader sets a five-slot sentence (who · did what · how much · over which games · against whom) and the board ranks every career, or every player-season, by its value at that window; a row opens inline under itself and reads against a peer; a player's page lists every comparison he leads. It is the NFL Comparator (`~/Projects/nfl-comparator`, player pages and the records mine) and the WNBA Comparator (`~/Projects/wnba-comparator`, the one-ledger page) over a basketball ledger six times the WNBA's; the planning spec is in the CFB repo, `cfb-streak-king/specs/nfl-career-point.spec.md`, and the NFL repo's `specs/player-pages.spec.md` is the records method. Keep the three engines in step when one learns something.
- Data: three sources. Before 1993-94, NBA.com's own game logs (`leaguegamelog`, 1946-47 to 1995-96, read once on a Mac by `npm run fetch:history`; the raw JSONs are gitignored; Drew chose them over the CC0 copy on 2026-10-21) patched from Eoin A. Moore's CC0 Kaggle republication of the same data, converted by `npm run history` (`scripts/build-history.mjs`) into the committed `data/history/` files (`player_games.csv.gz`, `people.csv.gz`), so CI never calls NBA.com. From 1993-94 on, hoopR (sportsdataverse) release files, CC BY 4.0, two pipelines: the spine is the ESPN box scores (`player_box_<season>.csv`, 1993-94 on: a row per player per game with the line, the result and the starter flag); the NBA Stats box scores (`player_boxscores_<season>.csv` with `nba_schedule_<season>.csv.gz` for the dates, 1996-97 on) fill the games and rows ESPN lacks and name the starters where ESPN's flag is short, joined through hoopR's crosswalk (`nba_player_crosswalk_<season>.csv`, 2001-02 on) and by name before that; the ESPN bios (`player_core_<season>.csv`) give birth dates, positions and draft years, the NBA Stats rosters (`rosters_<season>.csv`) positions, birth dates and the years of experience that say who began before 1993-94. `npm run fetch` (`scripts/fetch-hoopr.mjs`) downloads them into `data/raw/` (gitignored, 600 MB; the running season's files refetch after a day; its NBA Stats files are optional because that pipeline lags ESPN's). `npm run ledger` (`scripts/build-ledger.mjs`, about 20 s) writes the ledger, committed, so the site builds without the raw files. Seasons are named by the year they end in everywhere in code (1994 is 1993-94); `seasonWords` makes the words. `scripts/lib/window.mjs` holds the first (1947), ESPN-first (1994) and current season and is the only place a year is typed. The history builder corrects NBA.com's logs in the open (`data/ref/history-corrections.json` with receipts, then four mechanical passes; AGENTS.md lists them and the residue the audit of 2026-10-21 left). Careers that cross 1993-94 are joined to one id by the crosswalk, the NBA Stats rows and the box lines of 1993-94 to 1995-96 (AGENTS.md has the join).
- The ledger is columnar (`src/lib/ledger.ts` decodes it): `src/data/ledger.json` is the players (id, name, birth date, debut, `censored`, position and its source) each with its slice `[lo, hi)` of the game arrays, and `src/data/cols/<name>.bin.gz` is one gzipped typed array per column over every game (the day, the flags, the team, the team's game number, the opponent, sixteen stats). A game is an index; `gameAt` builds an object only for display. The page bundles `ledger.json`, fetches the four base columns and the default board's `pts` before it hydrates (`src/main.jsx`, about 1.5 MB gzipped), and fetches the columns a query reads as the query needs them (`queryCols`, `ensureCols` in `src/lib/load.ts`, `src/ledger-client.js` for the asset URLs), keeping the previous board on screen meanwhile. 860,000 player-games as objects would cost a phone two hundred megabytes and overflow the share worker; this is why the NBA engine reads `(L, i)` where the siblings read a `Game`.
- Rulings the words rest on (also in `Notes.jsx`): a game is an appearance (a row with minutes or points); a start is ESPN's flag, so the starts fields begin in 1993-94, NBA Stats' first five rows stand in where ESPN flagged four or six, and the three team-games nobody can name the starters of sit a career out of the starts fields when one falls inside its window (`board.gapped`); a stat counts from the season it is in every box (`Stat.since`: steals, blocks, turnovers, minutes and offensive/defensive rebounds 1983-84, threes 1979-80, assists 1975-76, rebounds and field-goal attempts 1969-70, fouls 1951-52, points and free throws 1946-47), a career or season from before it sits out (`board.early`), a window with an unrecorded game sits out (`board.partial`), and the words carry the era the data forces (`eraBound`); a triple-double counts the tracked stats; a win is the result of a game in the sequence; the play-in and the NBA Cup final are out, the Cup's other games and the playoffs are in, the All-Star game is out; the regular season is the default and `scope=all` adds the playoffs; a position is the NBA roster's, else ESPN's; where the two sources disagree on a line NBA Stats stands; ranks are competition ranks with ties.
- The known-answer gate is `src/lib/query.test.ts` (and `npm run gate` prints the same numbers): Jordan's 1,072 games and 32,292 points, Bird's 897/21,791, Magic's 906/17,707, Kareem's 1,560/38,387, Wilt's 1,045/31,417 (NBA.com's book; BR says 31,419) and his 100 on Mar 2, 1962, Wilt's 3,748 through 100 games and 30,000 in 941, Jordan's 63 at Boston and 30,000 in 960, Magic's 139 triple-doubles (the NBA says 138), Zion's 2,524 and Luka's 2,332 through 100 games since 1993-94, Wembanyama's 366 blocks and 225 threes (and 2,273 points where NBA.com says 2,278: the one publisher difference, worded in Method), the 10,000-point games (Luka 358, LeBron 368, Edwards 412), LeBron's 20,000th and 30,000th with their dates, the 1,000-three counts and Curry's 2,000th, the 80-point games and Drummond's 23 missed free throws, Westbrook's 42 and Jokić's 34 triple-doubles, Kidd's 107, Curry's 402 threes and Harden's 2,818 points, Carter-Williams's 9 steals, Griffin's 27 straight double-doubles, LeBron's 1,297 and Curry's 268-game streaks, Jordan's 2,491 in 1995-96 (filled from the logs), the era bounds and the starts rule, plus the audit of 2026-10-21's exact numbers: twenty careers' games and points, Bellamy's 3,122 and Mikan's 2,816 through 100 games, Harper's 243 steals, Mark Jackson's 1,020 assists, Wilt's 4,029 on an 82-game board (a finished season is whole), Stockton's 1,164, Eaton's 456, the Wilt/Kareem/Jordan/Harden/Love streaks, the triple-double counts, Mikan's 68 games of 1950-51 and the single February 2020 games. Add a gate for every factoid the site is asked to reproduce, with the publisher named and the difference noted where there is one.
- Rulings the engine adds: a clean-game test (`max: 0`, "no turnovers", "no fouls") holds only in a game the player had ten minutes or more (`played()`, `PLAYED_MIN` in `query.ts`, inside `holds()` so the board, the panel's ticks, the records mine and the worker agree).
- Code layout: `src/lib` is TypeScript (`npm run typecheck` checks it and the worker), components JSX. `ledger.ts` the format and decoder; `load.ts` the column fetching; `query.ts` the engine (`evaluate` → ranked rows, the ones still inside the window, the gapped and censored counts; `curve`, `crossings`, `toMatch` the peer primitives; `queryCols`); `stats.ts` the catalog (noun, thresholds, the columns a stat reads; `dd`/`td`/`fx5` are per-game 0/1s, `ftmiss` is for the futility boards); `words.ts` every sentence the page says, the record words included; `compare.ts` the comparison the panel shows; `share.ts` the words a link carries (`SITE`, `shareTitle`, `shareDescription(q, L, …)`, `claimWords`, `shareText`, `cardHeading`, `cardRows`, `isShared`, `playerLead`, `playerText`); `url.ts` the query in the URL (one param a slot, defaults omitted, `t` the open player, `vs` his peer, which rides only with `t`; `since` is a season by its end year); `records.ts` the mine (see its header for the N grid and the NBA knobs); `groups.ts` the team pages (`FRANCHISES` folds the ledger's codes of the day into the 30 franchises; `groupData` a page's rows and curves); `analytics.ts` the `track` helper. `pos` is a filter on the field (`ALL`, `G`, `F`, `C`), not a ledger.
- The page, top to bottom (the sports-site skill's page anatomy): the dateline (`drewhoover.com · 1993-94 – 2026-27 · updated`) with the icon bar (the player finder, Ko-fi, share); the h1; the sentence; the one footnote; the board with the open row's panel inline under its row (`Board.jsx` `brow-wrap`; the page extends so the open row is always rendered, a `?t=` link lands on the row after hydration, a position change that drops the open player out of the field closes him); What to read next (every sibling); "Using a comparison from here" with the citing ask and Ko-fi; Method (`id="method"`); the footer; comments. `document.title` is set client-side with the words the worker writes, and goes back to the page's own title at the bare address. The share button is Streak King's `useShare` (the native sheet where there is one, else copy "text url" and flash copied).
- Search (the pieces Streak King ships and the NFL spec planned, `~/Projects/nfl-comparator/specs/seo.spec.md`): the root `<title>` carries the default board's words; every board has one h1, the site's name as an eyebrow span and the board's subject worded the way it is searched as the title span (`h1Words` in `words.ts` for an unnamed query, the landing's own `h1` for a named one), which the prerender checks for on every named board; **named boards** (`src/lib/landing.ts`, 32 curated queries) each live at their own path (`/fastest-to-10000-points/`), prerendered as the board on that query with the searcher's phrase as the h1, the leader in a lead line, the era the data forces in the title, an ItemList of the top ten (links to player pages where they exist) and the worker's card as `og:image`; the path is the query's canonical address (`hrefOf` in `url.ts` writes it whenever the board is on that query; `parseAddress` reads a path plus its query string, so `/fastest-to-10000-points/?t=1035` is that board with Jordan open, and the worker rewrites such an address's head); `Landings.jsx` lists them under the board as real links; a player page's claim links the named path when its query is one (`hrefOf`), and the worker writes a named board's page as the canonical of a root address whose query is one, the root otherwise; player titles are the fact people type ("Michael Jordan: every comparison he leads at the same point in a career"); every leaderboard and player page carries ItemList JSON-LD (`scripts/lib/jsonld.mjs`); `dist/llms.txt` (`scripts/lib/llms.mjs`) describes the site, the named boards with their leaders and five player pages; `scripts/indexnow.mjs` (the key file is `public/<key>.txt`) pings Bing's IndexNow after every deploy from the `indexnow` job (the named boards and the root on a push, the whole sitemap on the scheduled refresh; `--dry` prints). Adding a named board: a line in `LANDINGS` (the slug is the address forever), and `landing.test.ts` pins the round trip.
- Player pages: `/player/<id>/` (`App.jsx` `parseRoute`, `PlayerPage.jsx`), one per player with a row above the records floor; `npm run records` (`scripts/build-records.mjs`) mines what every player leads and writes `public/players/<id>.json` plus `public/players/index.json` (every player, with `n` rows and `top` score; the finder's list and the panel's "what he leads →", shown only where a page exists); the prerender writes each page with its file embedded (`#player-data`, so the hydrate matches) and its own card (`scripts/lib/og-player.mjs`), and the sitemap. `?tune=1` opens the scoring tuner in a dev build. The finder's pick goes to the page when there is one, else opens the row on the board.
- Team pages: `/team/<team>/<guards|forwards|centers>/` (`GroupPage.jsx`, the College Football Comparator's group pages), 90 of them, every player with 82+ regular-season games for the franchise ranked by points, a cumulative chart per metric (G points/assists/3-pointers, F points/rebounds/assists, C points/rebounds/blocks, the top 8 of each) and the table; a total is the games whose box has the stat, starred where some lack it, blank where more than a tenth do. The prerender writes each with its data embedded (`#player-data`), its card (`scripts/lib/og-group.mjs`), WebPage + BreadcrumbList + ItemList JSON-LD and a sitemap line, and `/team/` lists them; `Landings.jsx` links the list. `groups.test.ts` is their gate. The Hornets of 1988-2002 are Charlotte's (the NBA's book since 2014), the New Orleans Hornets the Pelicans'.
- The era picker's cuts are the league's own (`ERAS` in `Sentence.jsx`: the shot clock 1954-55, the ABA merger 1976-77, the three-point line 1979-80, Jordan and the full box score 1984-85, zone defense 2001-02, the hand-check ban 2004-05, the three-point era 2014-15), each with its hint; a stat's own wall comes first when later. The Method is folded `<details>` sections (sources; what the box records and since when; the words; why the old names and the eras; corrections and residue) so a reader opens the one they came to argue about and a crawler reads them all.
- Ko-fi is `KOFI_URL` in `Notes.jsx`, three placements, each `track('kofi', { from })`: the icon bar (`icons board` / `icons player` / `icons team`), the notes line (`notes`), the footer (`footer`). No button, no badge, no orange.
- Look: College Football Streak King's (the dark stadium palette, Georgia Bold h1 and h2, Graduate for numbers, Source Serif for words, Plex Mono labels, dashed-underline pickers; `Picker.jsx` and `Icons.jsx` are copied from it). Every select is `Picker.jsx`; no native `<select>`. Rust is for data, never chrome.
- Vite + React static site for GitHub Pages under drewhoover.com: `package.json` "name" is the URL slug and must equal the repo name; `site.config.js` the title and OG copy (the year comes from `window.mjs`); `npm run build` is the records mine, `vite build` and `scripts/prerender.mjs` (the board, every player page with its card, the sitemap, and `dist/data/` for the worker), so `main.jsx` hydrates and nothing reads `window` during render. The repo will be `DrewHoo/nba-comparator`, live at `https://drewhoover.com/nba-comparator/`; `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` deploys on every push to `main` (the Pages build, then the worker job) and refetches on the daily 12:00 UTC schedule, caching `data/raw` across runs.
- Social previews: the share worker (`workers/share/`, name `nba-comparator-share`) sits on `drewhoover.com/nba-comparator/*`. For a board address whose query says more than the root it rewrites `<head>` with the words from `src/lib/share.ts` (a crawler's request also loads the ledger's columns for that query and names the leader, or the open player read against his peer) and points `og:image` at `/nba-comparator/share/og.png?<the same params>`, drawn with workers-og (`card.ts`: a board's top rows, or with a player open, the NFL Comparator's comparison card, his side and his peer's, the panel's curves and lines; every glyph inside the centered `SAFE` 720px column) from the columns the prerender publishes under `dist/data/` (raw-GitHub fallback); player pages pass through with their own static cards. `CARD_VERSION` in `index.ts` keys the edge cache: bump it with any change to what a card draws. Local: `npm run build`, `npx vite preview --port 4174`, `npm run worker:dev`, then `http://localhost:8788/nba-comparator/share/og.png?n=50`. The worker holds the ledger for the UTC day (about 8 MB resident plus a column per stat it has been asked for) and reloads it once if a column stops matching after a mid-day deploy.
- Dev server: `npm run dev` (the launch config runs it on 5177; the WNBA site is 5176, the NFL 5175). Tests: `npm test` (Vitest; `rtk proxy npx vitest run --reporter=verbose` to read the output). Images: `npm run gen:favicon` copies drewhoover.com's own favicon set; `npm run gen:og` draws `public/og.png` and `public/card.png` from the real default board with Georgia Bold from the Mac. Both are committed; re-run `gen:og` after a redesign or when a fresher board is worth committing, and open the PNGs.
