# Notes for the agent: what the data and the logic turned out to be

The idiosyncrasies the first build found, for whoever changes the ledger builder or the engine next. CLAUDE.md is the map; this is the running list of surprises. Add to it whenever a gate or a reader's correction teaches us one.

## The data

- **Three sources, two spines.** Before 1993-94 the spine is NBA.com's own game logs (stats.nba.com `leaguegamelog`, PlayerOrTeam=P, every season from 1946-47, folded franchises included; a stat that wasn't recorded is null and a minute count of 0 means unknown), read once on a Mac by `scripts/fetch-history.mjs` (`npm run fetch:history`, 2.5 s between requests; the 100 JSONs sit in `data/raw/history/`, gitignored; Drew's call of 2026-10-21 to use the league's logs over the CC0 copy) and patched with the rows NBA.com has since misplaced (Loy Vaught's first id, Kurt Rambis's 1990-91, Greg Smith's 1973-74: about 680 rows) from Eoin A. Moore's CC0 Kaggle republication of the same data, which writes every null as 0 and lacks the folded franchises before 1954-55, so it is the patch and not the spine. `scripts/build-history.mjs` turns them into `data/history/player_games.csv.gz` (695,638 rows through 1995-96, 10 MB) and `people.csv.gz` (birth dates, NBA.com's position flags), both committed, so CI never calls NBA.com. The history's 1993-94 to 1995-96 rows are read only to join and to fill. Nobody's career is censored now (the flag and the code path stay).
- **The join across 1993-94.** ESPN athlete ids and NBA person ids meet through the crosswalk (2001-02 on, weight 1000 a season), the NBA Stats rows the fill pass placed (1996-97 on), and the box lines of 1993-94 to 1995-96 (the same points, rebounds, assists and field-goal attempts on the same day is the same man: 67,150 matches). The heaviest pairs win, one id each way; 3,062 persons map. Jordan (1,072 regular-season games, 32,292 points), Stockton, Malone, Shaq, Barkley and Pippen each come out as one career. What's left split: Kurt Rambis's one 1990-91 row (NBA.com's own split id) and Corey Williams (two person ids, 35 and 39 games). The 12 whole games and 266 rows ESPN lacks in 1993-94 to 1995-96 are filled from the history, which is how Jordan's 1995-96 reads 2,491 again.
- **Where the box score fills in** (`Stat.since` in stats.ts, from the logs' per-row null rates): points, field goals made and free throws from 1946-47; fouls 1951-52; field-goal attempts and rebounds for nearly every game from 1969-70; assists 1975-76 (5–8% null 1968-73, under 2% after); threes 1979-80, complete; minutes, offensive and defensive rebounds, steals, blocks and turnovers in every game from 1983-84 (tracked 1973-74 and 1977-78, 30–70% null until 1981-82). The column byte 255 (`UNK`) is "not recorded"; `Stat.read` returns `UNKNOWN` (-1); a window with an unknown sits out (`Board.partial`), a career or season from before the stat's era sits out (`Board.early`), and the board's `since` is the era its words carry (`eraBound`). A double-double or triple-double counts the tracked stats, as the record books do (unrecorded steals and blocks are none), so Magic reads 139 triple-doubles (the NBA says 138) and Oscar sits out (his career began before assists were in every box; the record books give him 181). Wilt reads 31,417 points to Basketball-Reference's 31,419: NBA.com's book, kept as is.
- **What the history builder corrects, and what the audit of 2026-10-21 found.** Two Opus audits checked ~200 famous numbers across every era against Basketball-Reference's game logs (the scripts and saved receipts are in that session's scratch; the exact ones are gates in `query.test.ts`). Careers from 1993-94 on match BR exactly in every counting stat once four late-night West Coast games of February 2020 (ESPN dated them to the next UTC day, so the NBA Stats fill added a second copy: Jokić, Carter and Anthony each read a game high) and ESPN's did-not-play rows written as lines of zeros (Harden's 2023-10-31 under the Clippers, 84 rows in 2023-24) are handled, both in `build-ledger.mjs`. Minutes after 1993-94 drift a few per career because ESPN rounds them. Before 1993-94, `build-history.mjs` applies, in order: the receipts in `data/ref/history-corrections.json` (a Schayes row BR doesn't have; the six 1947–57 playoff tiebreakers NBA.com types as regular season, which put Pettit at 792/20,880 and Kerr's streak at 844); rows wedged between another team's games (another man's lines under a player's id: Ed Mikan's 25 games under George in 1950-51, Dwight Jones's Houston rows during his 1979-80 Chicago season, Johnny Davis's, Michael Cooper's; pass A takes point-only rows among a season of full lines, pass B the smaller stint's rows inside the bigger stint's games, three days either side); one man on two teams in one day (the team of his nearest other game stays; a doubleheader for one team is two games); a stat at 0 for every player of a team-game (unrecorded); and a line carried twice in a team-game under two ids where the team's points exceed its score by that line (the man with fewer games on that team that season is the ghost). The same man twice in one game keeps the fuller row (Cousy's 13 over a blank Baltimore row). Residue: 8 of Dwight Jones's fake Houston rows at the edge of his trade; about one pre-1994 team-game in a hundred whose player points miss the team score (256 over, 333 under; NBA.com's book); whole team-games from 1969-70 to 1981-82 with no box beyond points (Moses Malone's 1976-11-06, Kareem's 24 games without fouls, Erving's two, Gilmore's two), which sit those careers out of the full-box fields; NBA.com lines BR reads differently (West 39 vs 36 on 1963-01-31, Magic's 12 rebounds on 1988-01-12 that make his 139th triple-double, Hakeem's 0 blocks on 1985-10-31); and BR's own season totals disagreeing with its game logs in the 1980s (Alvin Robertson's 301 steals are 300 in the logs, and ours).
- **A finished season fills a longer window.** A `teamGames` window of 82 over a 75-game 1959-60 is the whole season (`complete` when the season is over), worded "all 75 of PHW's games of 1959-60"; the records mine reads the same (`whole` in `buildCtx`). Without it, no season before 1967-68 could rank on an 82-game board and Wilt's 4,029 never appeared.
- **Old team codes** are NBA.com's of the day (PHW, SYR, FTW, MNL, CIN, SFW, KCK, SDC, NOJ, BUF, CAP, BAL…), with GOS→GSW, UTH→UTA, SAN→SAS, PHL→PHI and WAS→WSB through 1996-97 so the franchise reads the same on both sides of 1993-94. 71 codes fit the byte.
- **Two sources, one spine, from 1993-94.** hoopR (sportsdataverse) publishes ESPN box scores from 1993-94 (`player_box_<season>.csv`, a row per player per game with the starter flag) and NBA Stats box scores from 1996-97 (`player_boxscores_<season>.csv`, no dates: `nba_schedule_<season>.csv.gz` has them). ESPN is the spine because it starts three seasons earlier; NBA Stats fills what ESPN lacks. The WNBA site's spine tag (`*_stats_player_game_logs`) holds **team** rows for the NBA, not player rows.
- **Seasons are named by the year they end in** in every source's file names and `season` columns (2027 is 2026-27), and the page says "1993-94" (`seasonWords`). The season rides in its own column (`season.bin.gz`, the year less 1900): no date cutoff works across eighty years (1967-68 opened on October 13 and the 2020 Finals ended on October 11). The `day` column counts from 1946-01-01. The running season's NBA Stats files appear weeks after ESPN's, so they are optional in the fetch.
- **What ESPN lacks.** About 68 whole regular-season games, clustered in the 1990s at Utah, Washington and Cleveland (game ids ending 100, 072, 082), 27 of them in 1999-00; a few hundred single rows, mostly sub-minute appearances (Mario West, Charles Jones) that ESPN shows as blanks; and the starters of about 500 team-games in 2002-07 (Miami and Indiana 2005-07 above all), where its flag marks four or six. NBA Stats fills the games and rows from 1996-97 and names the starters by its row order (the first five rows of a team are the starters, 2,621 of 2,632 team-games in 2015-16; the misses are crosswalk gaps). The games of 1993-94 to 1995-96 ESPN lacks are filled from NBA.com's logs (data/history), which is how Jordan's 1995-96 reads 2,491. Three team-games keep unknown starters.
- **Where the two disagree on a line, NBA Stats stands** (about 300 rows, most in 2016-17 and 2020-21). Minutes are compared loosely: ESPN rounds to the minute, NBA Stats gives MM:SS.
- **A game is an appearance**: a row with minutes or points. `did_not_play` is unreliable in old seasons (injured players appear as `false` with every stat blank), so blank-everything is the DNP test. In 2025-26 46 rows have points (0) and no minutes; they count as appearances.
- **The All-Star game sits in `season_type` 2** with team ids above 30 (EAST/WEST, STARS/STRIPES, LEB/STE…), typed `ALLSTAR` in most years but `STD` in 2016. The team-id rule removes it. The **NBA Cup final** is `type_abbreviation` CC (type 39) in season_type 2 from 2023-24 and is out; the Cup's other games are ordinary regular-season games. The **play-in** is season_type 5 (one game in 2020, six a year from 2021) and is out: neither regular season nor playoffs in the league's own books. Postponed games appear as extra `STATUS_POSTPONED` rows with their own ids; only `STATUS_FINAL` counts. The 1999 and 2000 schedules carry ghost Chicago games (0-0, `player_box=false`) and the 2000 file repeats the whole 1999 lockout season; filtering on the file's `season` and on a score removes them.
- **Team codes.** ESPN's `team_id` is the franchise key; its abbreviation is the code of the day in ESPN style (GS, NY, NO, SA, UTAH, WSH). The ledger uses the familiar codes, by id and season (`codeOf`): CHH for the first Hornets through 2001-02, NOH for the New Orleans Hornets through 2012-13 (ESPN never writes NOK), NOP since; CHA for the Bobcats and the Hornets since 2004-05; NJN/BKN, SEA/OKC, VAN/MEM, WSB/WAS. **The Charlotte trap**: ESPN's `CHA` is id 3 (today's Pelicans lineage) before 2002-03 and id 30 from 2004-05; key on `team_id` + season, never the abbreviation. ESPN follows the 1988 Hornets to New Orleans; the NBA gave that history back to Charlotte in 2014; the ledger follows the data (CHH is its own code).
- **Ids.** ESPN's `athlete_id` is the player key and is stable across seasons (LeBron 1966, Kobe 110, Wembanyama 5104157). The ESPN ↔ NBA Stats crosswalk (`nba_player_crosswalk_<season>.csv`) starts in 2001-02; before that, and for its gaps, an NBA Stats row is placed by full name, then by the player on that team that season whose name shares the most tokens (Nene vs Nene Hilario, Yao Ming vs Ming Yao, Didier Ilunga-Mbenga vs DJ Mbenga, two Larry Johnsons). A matched player already in the game means the row is his and adds nothing. Two players exist only in NBA Stats (`nba:<person_id>` ids): Marcus Vinicius's 26 games and a one-game Ryan Robertson. Names are ESPN's latest spelling, trimmed, except where the NBA roster carries the accents ESPN drops (Dončić, Jokić, Porziņģis) for the same name.
- **Positions**: the NBA Stats roster's label, one vote a season (G/F/C and combos; F-G folds to F, G-F to G, PG/SG to G, SF/PF to F), else ESPN's. ESPN's label is the player's profile position *today*, applied to every season (Kobe reads SF in every ESPN row), so it is the second source. Two players have none. ESPN granularity varies by era (G/F/C in 1993-94, PG/SG/SF/PF around 2004, G/F/C again now).
- **Birth dates**: ESPN's bios (`player_core`), complete from about 2001-02 and thin before (287 of the 405 players of 1993-94 have no ESPN bio), then the NBA Stats roster's (`"DEC 14, 1967"`), which covers 1996-97 on. 273 players have none, nearly all with careers that ended before 1996-97.
- **`censored`** is now always false: the ledger begins with the league. The flag and `Board.censored` stay for a ledger that doesn't.
- **Season lengths**: 82 normally; 50 in 1998-99, 66 in 2011-12, 64 to 75 in 2019-20 (the bubble), 72 in 2020-21. A `teamGames` window of 82 leaves those seasons "still inside the window".
- **The 1,000-point check**: the gate reproduces the published number for every factoid it carries except one. NBA.com says Victor Wembanyama had 2,278 points through 100 games; both box scores say 2,273. The Method says so rather than patching it.
- **Dates** are ESPN's `game_date`, the US-Eastern local date (a 10:30 pm ET tip is dated that day). `game_date_time` changes format by season and isn't used.
- **The format**: the ledger is columnar because the NBA is 1.48 million player-games (the WNBA's 136,000 as objects were fine; six times that is not, in a phone or in the share worker's 128 MB). `ledger.json` has the players with their slices; `cols/<name>.bin.gz` one gzipped byte (or Uint16 for `day`) per game per column, inflated with `DecompressionStream`. GitHub Pages serves `.gz` without recompressing it, which is the point. Stat values are clipped to a byte and the builder throws if any exceeds it (none does; 83 points, 69 minutes).

## The logic

- Four sequences: a career's games, its starts (ESPN's flag, fixed from NBA Stats where ESPN flagged four or six), its seasons, and a team's games of a season (the team's schedule, so a missed game is a gap the window still counts, and a mid-season trade is read on the first team's schedule).
- A clean-game test (`max: 0`) needs ten minutes on the floor (`played`, `PLAYED_MIN`), inside `holds()` so the board, the panel's ticks, the records mine and the worker agree.
- A seasons window is complete when the Nth season is over; `reach` reuses `n` as the target and `every` ranks by the count; competition ranks with ties; crossings are entries into a tie or a lead, and `toMatch` is arithmetic.
- `queryCols(q)` names the columns a query reads; `evaluate` throws if one is missing, and the page (`App.jsx`) loads them first (`ensureCols`), keeping the previous board on screen meanwhile. The default board needs about 2.4 MB gzipped (the five base columns and `pts`); everything is 11 MB. If that hurts, delta-encode `day` and `tgn`.
- The records mine (`records.ts`) walks an N grid rather than every N, because careers run to 1,643 games; see the file's header for the grid and the per-sport knobs (tiers, milestones, components, the minutes rule for a player-season being in the field).

## Working here

- `rtk` rewrites vitest's output and its parser fails on it: `rtk proxy npx vitest run --reporter=verbose`.
- Node 22 strips types, so `scripts/*.mjs` import `src/lib/*.ts` directly; `scripts/lib/ledger-node.mjs` loads the whole ledger for a script.
- The ledger build wants heap: `npm run ledger` passes `--max-old-space-size`; it takes about 20 s and 34 CSVs (600 MB) in `data/raw`.
- `npm run typecheck` is two passes (the root, then `workers/share`): the DOM lib and the Workers types collide on `CacheStorage`.
- `wrangler` and `opentype.js` are pinned (4.151.3 and 1.3.4); Georgia Bold comes from the Mac and is never committed; CI's cards fall back to Graduate.
- A named board's path is served by GitHub Pages as a directory index; on the dev server Vite's SPA fallback serves `index.html` and `App.jsx` reads the slug from `window.location.pathname` in `main.jsx`, never in render.
- The dev server is `.claude/launch.json`'s `nba-comparator`, port 5177 (the WNBA site is 5176, the NFL 5175); preview 4174; worker 8788.

## The records mine (player pages)

- The method is NFL's (`~/Projects/nfl-comparator/specs/player-pages.spec.md`); `records.ts` walks an N grid (every N to 100, every 5 to 500, every 10 beyond for games and starts; every N to 82 for team games; every N to 25 for seasons) because careers run to 1,797 games, and builds each sequence's field once for every stat. 1,179 shapes mine in about 22 s on one thread (`npm run records`); 1,115 of 5,122 players get a page (5.4 MB of files, 1 MB gzipped). An entity's values are `POISON` from the first game with an unrecorded stat on, and for a whole career or season from before the stat's era, so the mine sits them out exactly as the board does; the field at each step is filtered for it.
- Where it departs from NFL: a lead that starts before `minN` is trimmed to it, not dropped (Wembanyama's begin at game 1); a set row needs ten careers in the field when he got there, or the 1993-94 debut class "sets" every deep record by reaching it first (Van Exel had 1,411 such rows); a streak of one is no row; a player-season's date at team game N is the team's game N, not his own last game; the starts sequence ends at the first start-unknown game.
- A row's field is every player (`pos: 'ALL'`); the page says "among players" whatever his position. Count and streak rows outscore sums for a family's face, as on NFL; the headline totals sit under them. The tuner (`?tune=1`) is for that.
- "Active" and "live" mean played in the running season: at the start of a season that is a few dozen players, so most pages show no live rows until November.
- `records.test.ts` checks a held row's value against `evaluate` at its worded N; it is what catches the mine and the board drifting apart. 120 random held rows matched when it was built.
